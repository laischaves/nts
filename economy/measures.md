### VaR
Can be either:
- Value at risk
- Variance

Value at risk is usually quoted in USD for a given probability and time range:
> 1% one year VaR of $10 million means 1% chance of a portfolio losing 10% million in a year.

### Stress test
Is a method of assessing the risk for a firm's portfolio -- can be ordered by a government agency or regulators. Is basically a stress scenario assessment.


### β (Beta)
The beta of a stock is a measure of how it relates to the stock market. It's a regression slope coefficient when the _ith_ return on the asset is regressed on the return market.

### Solvency
Is the market value of assets larger than that of the liabilities of a firm.

### Liquidity
Are the money reserves or the ability to acquire them by a firm.

### Net Present Value (NPV)
The value of all future cash flows over the entire life of an investment discounted to the present.

$$NPV = FV \times \frac{1}{(1+i)^n}$$
With _i_ = discount rate (cost of capital) and _n_ = period.

E.g.:

| **n**  | 1    | 2    | 3    | 4    | 5    | expected final value (n = last n) |
| ------ | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | ---- | --------------------------------- |
| **FV** | 2019 | 2020 | 2021 | 2022 | 2023 | 2024...                           |
| **$**  | 100  | 100  | 100  | 100  | 100  | 1200                              |

$$\text{future value}\cdot \frac{1}{(1+\text{discount rate})^\text{period}}$$

Considering an arbitrary discount rate of _0.01_ (1%):

$$2019 = (\frac{1}{1+0.01})^1 \approx 91$$
$$2020 = (\frac{1}{1+0.01})^2 \approx 83$$
$$\Large{\vdots}$$
**Present value** = 91+83+75+68+62+745 = _1124_
